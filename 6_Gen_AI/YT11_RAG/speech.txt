World War II (1939–1945)

World War II was one of the largest and most devastating conflicts in human history. It began in 1939 when Germany, led by Adolf Hitler, invaded Poland. This action caused Britain and France to declare war on Germany, starting a global conflict that involved many countries.

Two main alliances fought in the war. The Allies included countries such as the United States, the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, and China. The Axis powers were mainly Germany, Italy, and Japan.

The war was fought across Europe, Asia, Africa, and the Pacific Ocean. Major events included the Battle of Stalingrad, the attack on Pearl Harbor in 1941, and the D-Day invasion in 1944. These battles played a crucial role in weakening the Axis powers.

World War II ended in 1945. Germany surrendered in May 1945 after Allied forces captured Berlin. Japan surrendered in September 1945 after the United States dropped atomic bombs on the cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

The war caused the deaths of around 70–85 million people, making it the deadliest conflict in history. After the war, the United Nations was formed to promote peace and prevent future global conflicts.
